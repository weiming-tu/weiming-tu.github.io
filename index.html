The Tu Lab

Engineering biology for sustainability: turning CO2, sunlight and waste into fuels, chemicals and materials.

We redesign microbial cells so they can run on light and electricity instead of sugar, and make useful molecules out of carbon dioxide and waste.

The lab works at the meeting point of synthetic biology, environmental biotechnology and machine learning. We install light-harvesting rhodopsins and electron-transfer pathways into bacteria, turn the nanoscale periplasm into a confined "bio-nanoreactor" for solar fuels, and use machine learning to balance the metabolic pathways that follow. The same toolkit is applied to waste streams: sludge, food waste and dairy by-products become bioplastics and platform chemicals. The group is based in the School of Civil and Environmental Engineering and at NEWRI (the Nanyang Environment and Water Research Institute), Nanyang Technological University.

Research

Four directions, one carbon cycle

Capture the energy, wire it into a cell, direct the metabolism that uses it, and close the loop with waste. Each direction below is one step of that cycle.

01

Artificial photosynthesis

Microbial rhodopsin is the simplest light-harvesting system in nature. We install it into non-photosynthetic bacteria such as Escherichia coli and Ralstonia eutropha, giving cells phototrophy they never had, and couple it to extracellular electron transfer. The resulting artificial photosynthesis system reached a photosynthetic efficiency over five times that of natural chlorophyll-based photosynthesis.

Rhodopsin light harvesting · CO2 fixation · photo-electrosynthesis

02

Bio-nanoreactors for solar fuels

The bacterial periplasm is a narrow gap between two membranes. Confining hydrogenase, substrates, proton movement and electron transfer inside it turns the space into a "bio-nanoreactor". Pairing proton-pumping rhodopsin with iron sulfide nanoparticles achieved hydrogen production at an efficiency comparable to costly metal catalysts.

Whole-cell biocatalysis · biohydrogen · solar fuels
Engineering bionanoreactor in bacteria for efficient hydrogen production · PNAS, 2024 Rhodopsin-based light-harvesting system for sustainable synthetic biology · Microbial Biotechnology, 2024 Ongoing work extends this towards CO2 fixation into C1 compounds such as formate and methanol, powered by sunlight.
03

Machine learning for metabolic engineering

An engineered pathway has more possible configurations than can be tested by hand. We use machine learning to predict and optimise metabolic fluxes, then test the shortlist at the bench, so a bioprocess is designed rather than discovered.

Pathway balancing · metabolic flux optimisation · design–build–test–learn
Machine learning-driven optimization of metabolic balance for β-carotene production · Metabolic Engineering, 2026 From xylose to biobased high-value chemicals: advances in microbial xylose utilization for industrial biotechnology · Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry, 2026
04

Waste to materials

Sludge, food waste and dairy by-products are carbon sources somebody has already paid for. We engineer microbial cultures to convert them into bioplastics (polyhydroxyalkanoates) and platform chemicals, and at MIT engineered the oleaginous yeast Yarrowia lipolytica to convert acid whey into carotenoids.
